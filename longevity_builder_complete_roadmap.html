User data safety — this is critical for health apps
Health data is the most sensitive data that exists. You must design for privacy from day one, not add it later.
1
Supabase Row Level Security (RLS) — Every user can only see their own data. Built-in, free, one line of SQL to enable. Non-negotiable.
2
Never send raw health data to AI APIs. Anonymize before sending. Send "user has HbA1c of 6.8" not the user's name or email.
3
No data selling, ever. Since you're free + open source, write a 1-page privacy policy stating: data is never sold, never shared, stored encrypted. This is your trust moat.
4
DPDP Act compliance (India 2023) — India's new Digital Personal Data Protection Act requires consent, purpose limitation, and data minimization. Read the basics at meity.gov.in
5
Medical disclaimer — Always display: "This is not medical advice. Consult your doctor." Required legally and ethically. Never position the app as a diagnostic tool.
6
Optional: local-first architecture — Store sensitive data only on the user's device. Use localStorage + export. No server sees their health data at all. This is the gold standard for trust.

South Asian longevity — the numbers that matter
Indians develop type 2 diabetes at a BMI of 23–25 kg/m² — the same level where Europeans are considered healthy. This is Dr. Mohan's most important finding and it changes everything about how South Asians should approach aging.
Indian-specific longevity biomarkers
→
Healthy BMI for Indians: 18–22.9 (not 25 like Western charts)
→
Waist cut-off: Men <90cm, Women <80cm (visceral fat risk in South Asians is higher at same waist size)
→
HbA1c target: Under 5.6% (prediabetes in Indians begins earlier than in Westerners)
→
Fasting glucose: Under 90 mg/dL (not 100, which is the Western standard)
→
Postprandial glucose (2hr after rice meal): Under 120 mg/dL — this is where most Indians fail silently
Indian foods with clinical longevity evidence
🫐
Amla (Indian gooseberry)
Highest natural vitamin C source. Reduces LDL by 12–15% in 3 months (Thilakchand et al). One amla daily = real cardiovascular protection.
🍛
Dal (lentils)
Low glycemic index (18–29). High in resistant starch — feeds gut microbiome. 1 cup/day associated with 20% lower cardiovascular mortality (Dahl et al).
🌿
Turmeric (curcumin)
NF-kB pathway inhibitor — reduces systemic inflammation. 500mg curcumin with black pepper (piperine) daily shows CRP reduction in RCTs. Cook with fat for absorption.
🌰
Almonds (soaked)
23 almonds = 6g protein, magnesium, vitamin E. Reduces postprandial glucose spike when eaten before rice meals. ICMR recommends as snack replacement.
🫚
Ghee (in moderation)
Contains butyrate — a short-chain fatty acid that feeds colon cells and reduces inflammation. 1 tsp/day is protective, not harmful. Fear of ghee is largely Western bias.
🍚
Rice (eaten cold)
Cooling cooked rice for 12 hrs increases resistant starch by 10×, lowering glycemic index significantly. Ancient Indian practice of eating previous day's rice was nutritionally sound.
Daily rhythm grounded in clinical evidence
6–7am
Wake with sunlight. 10 min outdoor light exposure sets circadian rhythm. Lowers cortisol spike. Indian tradition of surya namaskar has this built in.
7am
Amla + warm water. Empty stomach. Delays gastric emptying, reduces morning glucose spike.
8–9am
Breakfast: eggs or dal + 4 soaked almonds. High protein, low GI. Reduces total caloric intake for the day by 20% (satiety effect).
10min walk after lunch
Non-negotiable. A 10-min walk after rice reduces 2hr postprandial glucose by 20–30%. Most impactful single habit for South Asians per Dr. Mohan's clinical data.
Lunch: 12–1pm
Dal + sabzi + small rice portion. Eat dal first (protein buffer), then vegetables, then rice. Order matters — reduces glucose spike by up to 38% (Shukla et al, Cornell).
Sunset
Finish eating 3 hrs before sleep. Aligns with circadian insulin sensitivity. Ancient Indian practice of early dinner (before 7pm) was clinically correct.
10pm
Sleep with dark, cool room. 18–20°C is optimal. Indians often sleep in warm rooms — this suppresses melatonin and raises HbA1c over time.
What each DeepMind tool gives you — practically
AF
AlphaFold (alphafold.ebi.ac.uk) — Free database of 200M+ protein structures. You can look up FOXO3, SIRT1, mTOR — proteins that control aging — and see exactly how supplements or compounds interact with them. No API needed. Just download the PDB file and use Gemini to explain it in plain language.
AM
AlphaMissense — Predicts whether a genetic variant is harmful or benign. If a user uploads their 23andMe data, you can cross-reference their variants against AlphaMissense to flag aging-related risk. Dataset is free on GitHub.
AG
AlphaGenome (2025, new) — Predicts how DNA sequences affect gene expression. This is the tool for understanding how lifestyle changes gene activity — the core of epigenetic longevity. Still research-grade but datasets are public.
G2
Gemini 2.5 (your main tool) — Free via AI Studio. You use this as the brain of your app — reading research, interpreting biomarkers, generating personalized advice. 1M token context means you can feed it entire research papers.
